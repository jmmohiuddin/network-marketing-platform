Compliance Evidence Reports

Five reports computed from a company's own back-office export: who the sales went to, what the multi-level pay was earned on, what the money was paid for, how activity was reached, and what everyone actually earned.

Synthetic data. Every figure below comes from an invented company ("Demo Co.", 1,466 participants, 12 months) generated by a script. It describes no real business. These are indicators for a compliance reviewer, not findings that any plan is lawful or unlawful. Review thresholds are examples; your counsel sets yours.

Who the sales went to

R1
Method
  • Net sales = order amount minus refunds, assigned to the order's month.
  • "Customers" are participant types your plan defines as non-participants (here: customer, preferred customer).
  • "Declared resale" adds distributors' orders tagged for resale. A tag is a claim, so it is shown separately.
  • Data quality: the share of distributor orders with no purpose tag is reported, because unknown purpose weakens both figures.

What multi-level pay was earned on

R2
Method
  • Rewarded volume = volume of orders that generated at least one downline-volume payout line in the month.
  • Personal-use share = rewarded volume from distributors' own orders tagged for personal use.
  • Flagged payees: people whose downline-volume pay came more than one-third from personal-use orders. One-third is an example taken from the structure of one FTC order that binds one company; it is not a general legal test.

What the money was paid for

R3 ·

Method
  • Every payout line carries a source: a retail sale, downline volume, an enrolment order (starter kits), a pool, or a match.
  • Shares are of gross pay before clawbacks; clawbacks (refunds recovered from later pay) are shown separately.
  • Pay linked to enrolment orders is the figure reviewers usually ask about first.

How activity was reached

R4

Method
  • In this plan, "active" means at least 50 volume from your own orders in the month. So every active status depends on own purchases; the indicator that varies is how many active distributors served no customer that month.
  • Threshold orders: orders placed on day 27 or later that lifted a distributor's personal volume across the active (50) or leader (100) threshold. The report then shows how many were refunded in the next month.

What everyone earned in 2025

R5 · earnings disclosure

Annual earnings bandDistributorsShare
Method
  • Population: every distributor who held an agreement at any time in the year, including people who earned nothing. Leaving out zero earners is the most common way disclosures overstate typical income.
  • Earnings: gross commissions and bonuses after clawbacks, before any expenses the distributor paid.
  • The statement is computed from the payout ledger, so it can be reproduced for the same period later.

What a pilot would need from your system

Three files

An export of one year from your current back office, with these columns. Personal data can be replaced by IDs before it leaves your systems.

participants.csv  participant_id, type, enrolled_month, sponsor_id (servicing distributor for customers), market, status
orders.csv        order_id, month, day, buyer_id, amount_cents, volume_cents, purpose (customer | personal | resale | unknown | enrolment), is_starter_kit, refunded_cents, refund_month
payout_lines.csv  line_id, month, payee_id, bonus_type, source_kind (retail_sale | downline_volume | enrolment_order | pool | match | clawback | other), source_order_id, amount_cents
