### A.3 Recruitment and ethics (proposed)

- **Written consent**, with the option to remain anonymous; record audio only with consent; delete recordings after transcription.
- **Never recruit through "opportunity" or income messaging**, and offer no incentive that depends on purchasing, joining or recruiting.
- **Interview distributors independently of their company**: individual answers are never shared with the company or upline, and no one is asked to name their downline or share account credentials.
- **Collect only what is needed:** no account numbers, identity documents, passwords or confidential plan documents without a signed non-disclosure agreement.
- **Respect privacy law** in the participant's country (for example India's DPDP Act and Malaysia's data-protection law); keep notes minimal.
- **Do not promise income, products or business results**; do not discuss any real company's non-public information beyond what the participant volunteers.
- **Ask about behavior** ("the last time…") rather than opinions, and do not lead with the product idea.
- **Disclose the sponsor and purpose at first contact:** who the researcher is, that this is market research for a possible software product, and that nothing is being sold. Never present the research as something else.
- **Fieldwork materials** (routes, screeners, invitation and consent texts, moderator protocol, coding, tracker, pilot) are in **Appendix B**.

### A.4 Interview guides (draft; about 45 minutes each)

**G-EXEC (owner, CEO)**
1. How did you choose your current platform? Who started it, who signed, what almost stopped it, what did it cost (set-up and per year)?
2. When did you last change your compensation plan? Who did the work, how long did it take, what did it cost, what went wrong?
3. In the last 12 months, which request to your vendor or IT took longest?
4. Which markets do you operate in? Did you avoid or leave a market because of systems or rules?
5. Last time a regulator, bank, payment provider or lawyer asked for evidence about sales or earnings: what did you provide, who spent how long?
6. What would make you switch platforms? What stopped you the last time you considered it?
7. What do you spend each year on the platform and related services? Who owns the budget line? What would you need to see to consider a new compliance or reporting tool?
8. About what share of your sales goes to people who are not distributors, and how do you know?
9. Which one problem would you pay to fix first?

**G-FIN (finance, payout)**
1. Walk me through the last payout from approval to settlement.
2. The last time a payout was wrong, late or duplicated: what happened, how was it found and fixed, what did it cost in hours or money?
3. How do you handle a refund after commissions were paid? Last example, including any negative balance.
4. Chargebacks and card disputes on recurring orders: process and rough volume.
5. Which currencies, payment methods and providers do you use? What fails or costs most?
6. Tax forms by market: process, tools, last year's problems.
7. Month-end reconciliation: hours, tools, mismatches.
8. What do you check by hand that you wish the system did?

**G-OPS (commission or operations analyst)**
1. Describe a commission cycle from period close to payout.
2. About how many manual adjustments or exceptions were there in the last cycle, and why?
3. Last plan change: how was it tested? What would you want to simulate?
4. Last distributor dispute: what was it, how was it traced, how many hours?
5. Which systems, spreadsheets and scripts do you use? Which would you keep?

### A.6 Decision rules (proposed; agreed before fieldwork)

*"Operators" means companies interviewed. Thresholds are proposals and may be changed by the founder before fieldwork, but not after results arrive.*

| Hypothesis | Supported if | Refuted if | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H-4 / P-02** cost and speed of change | At least half of the interviewed operators on vendor platforms describe a plan change in the last 12 months that needed paid vendor work or a ticket **and** took longer than they consider acceptable, and at least 3 give a duration or cost | At least half say changes are fast and cheap | Inconclusive |
| **H-2, H-8 / P-05, P-12** compliance evidence and reversals | At least half of finance and compliance interviewees assembled evidence or handled reversals manually in the last 12 months **and** at least 3 give an owner and an effort estimate | At least half say existing reports and processes suffice with little effort | Inconclusive |
| **Commission errors are central (P-13)** | At least 3 operators describe an error affecting several payees in the last 24 months **with** quantified remediation (hours or money) | 1 or none, **and at least 5 operators answered the question** (the brief's assumption is then rejected) | Inconclusive (including exactly 2, or fewer than 5 answers) |
| **H-3 / P-03** local compliance packs | At least half of Malaysian and Indian operators handle local duties manually and none names a local tool that meets them | At least half name a local tool | Inconclusive |
| **H-1** price opacity | At least half could not compare prices when they bought **and** at least 3 report a cost surprise | Fewer than 3 cost surprises | Inconclusive |
| **Willingness to pay** | At least 3 operators with budget authority state current spend **and** name a budget they would use for the priority cluster | **Stop trigger:** one or none of 10 would evaluate or pay | Inconclusive |
| **Distributor-side problems (P-06 to P-10)** | No pass or fail; code responses as counts and quotes and re-rate in 07 by the same rubric | | |

**Implementation guards (added 2026-11-11 while building the checker; Appendix B §6):** a *Refuted* verdict for P-13 or H-1 needs at least 5 operators who answered the question; the willingness-to-pay stop trigger needs at least 10 who answered; blank answers are never read as "no"; pilot interviews are excluded; each company counts once. The founder may change these before fieldwork.

**Rules for the analysis:** two-pass coding to problem IDs; report negative cases; counts not percentages under 20; **if results contradict a rule above, the rule governs, not a later reinterpretation**; results update 06, 07, 05 and 66 and may reopen earlier gates.
