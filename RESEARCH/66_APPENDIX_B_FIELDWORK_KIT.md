## 5. Moderator protocol

**Structure (about 45 minutes):** introduction and consent (5); role and context (5); core questions from the guide in 66 A.4 (25 to 30); wrap-up and anything missed (5).

**Rules**
- **Ask about the last time** something happened, not what would or should happen.
- **Do not pitch or lead.** Do not describe the product idea until after the core questions, and then only to ask what is missing.
- **Ask for specifics:** how long, how many, who was involved, what it cost, how it was found.
- **Pricing questions come last** and are about current spend and budget, not hypothetical prices; stated intentions are weak evidence (66 A.5).
- **Neutral probes:** "Tell me more about that." "What happened next?" "How did you handle it?" "Who else was involved?" "How long did that take?" "Roughly what did it cost?" "How did you find out?"
- **Do not confirm or deny** facts about other companies or vendors.
- **Silence is allowed;** wait before the next question.

**Red lines**
- If asked for **legal or financial advice**, say you cannot give it.
- If the participant offers **confidential or personal data** (account numbers, credentials, a plan not covered by an agreement), stop them and do not record it.
- If a distributor describes **financial hardship or distress**, do not advise; thank them, shorten the interview and, if they ask, point to appropriate local consumer-protection or support resources. Never promise income.
- If a participant wants to **stop or withdraw**, end at once.

## 6. Coding and the decision-rule check

**Files in `r37_fieldwork/`:** `interview_log_template.csv` (one row per interview; Y, N or blank for NA); `pipeline_tracker_template.csv` (no personal data beyond a pseudonymous contact id); `r37_decision_check.py` (reads the log, prints counts and a verdict for each rule; run `python3 -I r37_decision_check.py interview_log.csv`).

**How the script applies the rules**
- **Pilots are excluded** (`is_pilot` = Y).
- **One value per company:** Y if any included respondent says Y; otherwise N if any says N; otherwise NA.
- **Role limits:** H-2 and H-8 use finance and compliance respondents only; the other rules use all four operator groups.
- **Counts, never percentages.** It also reports the sample against the targets and marks every verdict **PROVISIONAL** below 10 companies.

**A correction to 66 A.6, found while building the script.** The P-13 row in A.6 listed "1 or none" under Refuted but put "Not central; the brief's assumption is rejected" under Otherwise, which would have rejected the assumption on a count of exactly 2. **The corrected rule:** *Supported* if at least 3 companies; *Refuted* (the brief's assumption is rejected) if 1 or none **and at least 5 companies answered**; otherwise *Inconclusive*. 66 A.6 has been updated.

**Guards added so that silence is never read as a result** (they follow the spirit of A.6; you may change them before fieldwork):
- **P-13 and H-1:** a *Refuted* verdict needs at least 5 companies that answered the relevant question.
- **Willingness-to-pay stop trigger:** needs at least 10 companies that answered the "would you evaluate" question.
- **Conflicts:** if both *Supported* and *Refuted* conditions hold, the script prints CONFLICT for a person to review.

**Testing.** The script was run against a **synthetic test fixture** (invented rows, kept outside these deliverables) to check that each rule returns Supported, Refuted, Inconclusive, the stop trigger and the guards correctly, including blank answers and multiple respondents per company. **That test says nothing about the world.** The real log is empty.

**Distributor, leader and customer groups** have no pass or fail. Code responses as counts and quotes, then re-rate problems in 07 by its rubric.
